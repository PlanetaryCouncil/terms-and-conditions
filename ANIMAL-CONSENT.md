## The question posed by the artwork

The artist has stated personal willingness concerning a hypothetical human–animal sexual encounter, while acknowledging that an animal cannot give human informed consent. The artist has also left unspecified how the supposed animal consent would arise. This records the position expressed; it does not establish that animal consent exists or that an encounter is permissible.

That unresolved premise is a subject for critical discussion. A person's willingness addresses their own participation. It cannot supply another being's consent. Leaving a mechanism unspecified does not demonstrate that the mechanism is possible.

Animals cannot give informed consent in the human research sense: understanding the proposed intervention, its risks and alternatives, and making an informed, voluntary decision. This does not mean that animals lack preferences, agency, or the capacity to suffer. Behaviour indicating approach, avoidance, cooperation, or distress matters for welfare; it is not equivalent to informed consent. Lack of resistance is not proof of permission.

## Oversight is not consent

An **Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee (IACUC)** reviews animal care and use within the relevant US research framework. Its functions include protocol review, programme and facility evaluation, and addressing welfare concerns. Under PHS Policy, covered institutions must establish an IACUC and follow specified animal care and use requirements. The scope depends on the applicable regulatory and funding framework.

An IACUC does not speak an animal's informed consent into existence. Institutional approval is an oversight decision under a defined framework, not a universal permission slip. This page claims no IACUC approval for the artwork.

An owner's permission for veterinary care is likewise distinct from the animal's informed consent and does not create unlimited authority over the animal. “Consent by proxy” must not be used as an unexplained bridge between ordinary veterinary decisions and unrelated uses.

Sources: [Wikipedia overview supplied by the artist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institutional_Animal_Care_and_Use_Committee) and [NIH/OLAW: Public Health Service Policy on Humane Care and Use of Laboratory Animals](https://olaw.nih.gov/policies-laws/phs-policy.htm). Wikipedia is an introduction; the policy is the primary reference for the PHS framework. These US references do not determine the law everywhere.

## The 3Rs

Animal research ethics includes the **3Rs**:

- **Replacement:** use approaches that avoid or replace animal use.
- **Reduction:** use fewer animals while retaining sound scientific design.
- **Refinement:** minimise pain, suffering, distress, and lasting harm, and improve welfare.

These principles concern constraints on animal use, not a method for obtaining an animal's informed consent. For this artwork's inquiry, documentary research, discussion, and clearly labelled fiction provide ways to explore the question without involving an animal.

Source: [NC3Rs: the 3Rs](https://nc3rs.org.uk/the-3rs).

## Historical experiments and the development of safeguards

Historical use demonstrates what researchers did; it does not establish consent or justify repetition.

### Pavlov's research involving dogs

Ivan Pavlov's research on digestion involved dogs and invasive physiological experiments. His [1904 Nobel lecture](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/medicine/1904/pavlov/lecture/) is a primary historical account. It records scientific methods and the researcher's own assessment of them; it is not an independent welfare evaluation or evidence of the animals' consent. Scientific recognition and informed consent are separate questions.

### Pepper and the 1966 US legislation

The USDA National Agricultural Library documents the case of **Pepper**, a pet Dalmatian stolen in 1965 and sold to a research facility. Public concern about laboratory animal sourcing and treatment helped drive the legislation signed in 1966. This history concerns failures of protection and the development of safeguards, rather than a discovery of animal consent.

Source: [USDA National Agricultural Library: Animal Welfare Act timeline](https://www.nal.usda.gov/collections/exhibits/awahistory/list).

### Humane experimental technique and institutional oversight

Russell and Burch's *The Principles of Humane Experimental Technique* (1959) introduced the 3Rs. Later US developments included the Health Research Extension Act of 1985 and the 1986 PHS Policy edition. These are milestones in regulating human responsibility towards research animals.

Sources: [National Academies historical account](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/sites/books/NBK24662/?report=printable) and [NIH/OLAW policy history and text](https://olaw.nih.gov/policies-laws/phs-policy.htm).

These examples are selected historical context, not a comprehensive history of animal experimentation.

## Sde Teiman and documented abuse involving dogs

**Sde Teiman is a real detention facility, and abuse of Palestinian detainees, including the use of dogs to attack or intimidate people, is documented in human rights investigations. These subjects are not fictional inventions for this artwork.**

The [UN Commission of Inquiry's September 2024 report](https://www.un.org/unispal/document/report-of-the-independent-international-commission-of-inquiry-on-the-occupied-palestinian-territory-including-east-jerusalem-and-israel-11sep24/) documents sexual and gender-based violence in detention, including at Sde Teiman, and describes dogs used to intimidate and attack prisoners at named prisons. [Human Rights Watch's August 2024 investigation](https://www.hrw.org/news/2024/08/26/israel-palestinian-healthcare-workers-tortured) includes testimony from doctors held at Sde Teiman concerning dog-bite injuries to detainees.

These findings must be stated at their actual scope. Evidence of dog attacks does not by itself prove every specific allegation of sexual assault involving a dog, and accounts from different facilities must not be silently assigned to Sde Teiman. Attribution and evidence distinctions support accountability; they do not diminish survivors' experiences.

### Further reporting and the artist's compilation

The artist's related page, [“Attack dogs: torture and reported rape of Palestinian detainees”](https://warcrimes.planetarycouncil.org/dogs-torture/), collects published testimony, journalism, advocacy reporting, commentary, and satire. It includes allegations of sexual violence involving dogs against Palestinian detainees.

The linked compilation distinguishes published accounts from judicial findings. This page references that compilation without independently verifying each underlying allegation. Multiple publications repeating a testimony are not necessarily independent corroboration or separate incidents. Export records concerning dogs do not, by themselves, establish a particular assault.

Allegations of abuse warrant serious investigation. A hypothetical volunteer's willingness cannot establish or rebut what happened to a detainee. It does not supply a detainee's consent, and cannot retrospectively change an assault into a consensual encounter.
